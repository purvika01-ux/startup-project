# Ideas

## 1. Stock and trend tracker for small retail shops (lead idea)

**One-line pitch:** One-tap sales tracking that tells a shop owner when a trend is slowing down, how much to reorder, and when to run a clearance offer, before the stock goes dead.

**The business:** My father's shop in Ludhiana. It sells artificial jewellery, cosmetics and small girls' items (hairbands, claw clips, scrunchies). It gets 30–40 customers a day, mostly college girls and housewives, and buys wholesale from local wholesalers and from Delhi. Ludhiana has hundreds of shops like it.

**How they handle it today:**
- He decides what to buy from customer requests and a WhatsApp group of shop owners, which he says is about 50% accurate: wholesalers post their own stock, and Delhi taste isn't local taste.
- Quantity is a guess, and wholesalers push him to buy more. Only 1 in 3–4 bulk deals works out (e.g. 5 dozen claw clips, where the trend died in about a week).
- He keeps a register and writes bills by hand. The register only records totals, not items, so he spots a slowdown by eye, days late.
- Wholesalers don't take stock back. Dead stock gets sold at half rate or goes to the godown, which holds about ₹1.5–2 lakh of stock (never counted), some of it 3–4 years old.
- A billing app failed because entering 300 designs during rush hours took too long. When he tried buying less, he ran out.

**Who I'll talk to this week:** My father (follow-up visit), plus the two shop owners he referred me to.

## 2. Clinic token system

**One-line pitch:** A simple digital token queue so patients can see their turn on their phone instead of sitting in a crowded waiting room.

**The business:** Small doctor's clinics. Their waiting rooms are a visible problem, and clinics pay more readily than kiryana stores. Most of the system is already built.

**How they handle it today:** Patients' names are written in a register as they arrive. Then they wait in the clinic, often in long queues, with no idea when their turn will come.
