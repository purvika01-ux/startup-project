# Feedback: what owners said

## 1. My father's shop (Agra): artificial jewellery, cosmetics and girls' items

### About the business
- Sells artificial jewellery, cosmetics and small girls' items like hairbands and rubber bands.
- Buys wholesale from local wholesalers and from Delhi.
- About 30–40 customers a day, mostly college girls and housewives, buying a mix of small and big items, so daily sales vary.
- Records are kept in a register, and bills are handwritten.

### Round 1

**What he said:**
- **How he decides what to buy:** Based on what customers ask for in the shop, plus a WhatsApp community of shop owners where members share what's in high demand. Then he buys wholesale based on that.
- **How much to buy:** It's a guess, and the wholesaler pushes him to buy more. On his last buying trip he bought 5 dozen claw clips.
- **What happened next:** The trend died in about a week. He pointed to a box of scrunchies left over and said the extra stock was sold at half rate.
- **Dead stock:** Wholesalers don't take it back, so he takes the loss, and the godown is full of it.
- **General problems (from a broad question):** Competition, Meesho, rent.

**What I learned:**
- He guesses quantities, and the wholesaler pushes him to buy more.
- A trend can slow down within about a week.
- Wholesalers don't take stock back.
- The losses are real but not measured. "A godown full" is not a number.

**Review of my questions:**

| # | Question | Verdict | What I got |
|---|----------|---------|------------|
| 1 | "How many customers visit daily?" | Not leading, but a weak opener. Asking a number before introducing myself made him suspicious ("why?"). | A number, nothing useful |
| 2 | "I'm a student, what day-to-day problems do you face?" | Too broad. It also assumes he has problems, so he gave a stock complaint list. | Generic answers: competition, Meesho, rent |
| 3 | "How do shops like yours decide what stock to buy?" | Good framing, but it asks about shops in general, not what he actually did. | Experience, customer requests, the WhatsApp group |
| 4 | "On your last buying trip, how did you decide how much to buy?" | Best question. It's specific and about a real past event. | Quantity is a guess, wholesaler pressure, real numbers (5 dozen claw clips) |
| 5 | "What happened then?" | Excellent follow-up. It let him tell the story. | Trend died in about a week, the scrunchies box, the half-rate sale |
| 6 | "What do you do with the dead stock?" | Slightly leading: I used the phrase "dead stock" before he did. Still useful because he'd just pointed at the box. | No returns to the wholesaler, losses, a godown full of stock |

Leading questions: mostly #2, and mildly #6. Most of my questions were open.

### Round 2

**Overall:** A big improvement. All five questions were open, specific and non-leading, and each one got a useful answer. I closed well: I got a follow-up visit and two new contacts (shop owners he referred me to).

**Note:** I used the suggested follow-ups word for word. The next skill to build is writing my own follow-ups during the conversation, based on what he just said.

**What he said:**

| Topic | What I learned |
|-------|----------------|
| Wholesaler pressure | Only 1 in 3–4 bulk deals works out. He's caught between fear of dead stock and fear of running out. |
| When did you notice the slowdown? | He notices by eye, days late. The register records only totals, not items. |
| Money in the godown | About ₹1.5–2 lakh, never counted, some of it 3–4 years old |
| WhatsApp group | About 50% accurate. Wholesalers post their own stock, and local taste differs from Delhi. |
| Tried anything? | His nephew's billing app failed because entering 300 designs took too long during rush hours. Buying less made him run out. |

**Follow-ups I missed:**
- "If you don't take it and it becomes a hit, the shop next door sells. How often has that happened?" This is the other half of his problem. He fears running out as much as he fears dead stock.
- "The register only has the total. What would you want to know that it doesn't tell you?" Let him describe what he needs in his own words.
- "What would have made your nephew's app work for you?" He handed me a lesson about a failed product.
- "Your customers are college girls and housewives. Do they tell you what they want before you stock it?" Customer demand may be a better signal than the WhatsApp group.
- **Payment:** I never asked whether he would pay for a solution, or what he pays for anything today.

### What this means for the idea
- The shared trend platform looks weaker. He trusts the WhatsApp group only half the time, and his customers' taste is local.
- The real need is spotting slowdowns early and buying the right quantity, using his own shop's sales.
- Data entry has to be almost zero effort, like one tap or a photo. Typing 300 designs is what killed the last app.

### Still to clear up
- Is he a retailer, a wholesaler, or both? I told my partner he "sells to retailers," but I described it as a shop selling to customers. This changes who uses the product and what the right buying quantities are.
